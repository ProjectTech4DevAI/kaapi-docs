# Content Safety

**Runs on incoming messages**

Removes abusive or hurtful words from messages before the assistant reads them.

## What happens

**↻ Fixes & passes on** — The message continues as normal, with the abusive word hidden. The user is not interrupted.

## Example

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **User** | `Don't be such an <slur>, you know?` |
| **Assistant sees** | `Don't be such an [REDACTED_SLUR], you know?` |

The word is hidden before the assistant reads the message.

:::info

Works only when the incoming message is in English, Hindi or Hinglish. Messages in other languages pass through unchecked.

:::

## Without this guardrail

:::danger

The abusive word reaches the assistant, and may appear again in the reply. **Risk:** abusive language is not stopped, which hurts trust and looks bad for your organisation.

:::

## How well it works

| Accuracy | Response time |
| --- | --- |
| **78%** | **0.05 sec** |

Tested on 1,801 real-style messages in English and Hinglish.

<details open>
<summary>Technical details</summary>

| Method | Accuracy | Precision | Recall | F1 | Sample size |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rule + ML | 75% | 85% | 72% | 78% | 1,801 |

**Dataset:** [Evaluation dataset (1,801 samples) ↗](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1afTO6K19w1WLJtpe3D0hUfS4dH5Rbos4?usp=drive_link)

Evaluated on 1,801 real-style messages in English and Hinglish. Precision (85%) means flags are usually correct — about 1 in 7 flagged messages was actually clean; recall (72%) means about 1 in 4 real slur/profanity cases in this test set were missed. F1 (78%) balances the two.

</details>
